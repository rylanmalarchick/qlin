Cost model sources
==================
2026-09-27. One line per preset field: PRESET.field = value | tag | source.
Tags: calibration (a device calibration snapshot), paper (a published

HERON_KINGSTON (IBM Kingston, Heron r2)
  Calibration snapshot: qiskit-ibm-runtime FakeKingston, last_update_date
  2026-04-15T09:15:15+02:00, median over all qubits or pairs.

HERON_KINGSTON.t_1q = 32 | calibration | FakeKingston sx and x median, 32 ns
HERON_KINGSTON.t_2q = 68 | calibration | FakeKingston cz median, 68 ns
HERON_KINGSTON.t_3q = 408 | assumed | ccx as 6 cz (6 x 68 ns); the 1q gates of the decomposition are not counted
HERON_KINGSTON.t_meas = 1760 | calibration | FakeKingston measure_2 (mid-circuit measure) median, 1760 ns
HERON_KINGSTON.t_reset = 2312 | calibration | FakeKingston reset median, 2312 ns
HERON_KINGSTON.t_ff = 600 | paper | arXiv:2604.03360 (dynamarq), background section: feed-forward latency "approximately 600 ns on IBM hardware"
HERON_KINGSTON.t_branch = 0 | assumed | kept at 0 for the Phase 3 decision rule. Measured on ibm_kingston 2026-09-27: 3.45 us per if_else block, 95% CI 0.32 to 6.58 us (results/phase4_ibm.md)

Cross-checks and conflicts
  - Final measure on FakeKingston is 2280 ns. 2280 / 68 = 33.5, which
    agrees with dynamarq's "measurement durations 34x longer" than a 2q
    gate on Kingston.
  - dynamarq says the new mid-circuit measure is 65% shorter than the
    final measure, which gives 0.35 x 2280 = 798 ns. The snapshot gives
    1760 ns (23% shorter). The two disagree by more than 2x. The preset
    uses the snapshot, the device's own calibration. The paper and the
    snapshot may be from different calibration dates.
  - arXiv:2504.07258 (Heron devices, 2025): "the duration of a
    measurement ... between 3000 and 4000 ns, depending on which device
    is used" (measure and reset). Older than the snapshot. Not used.
  - The model has one t_meas. Programs here end with final measures
    too. Using the mid-circuit value for every measure underestimates
    the last layer by 2280 - 1760 = 520 ns per final measure.

T_BRANCH classes (2026-10-30): sweep points for t_branch, not presets.
  t_branch is controller time per branch decision, beyond the
  feedforward wait (t_ff). A switch takes 1 (table), one per bit (tree),
  or one per compare (chain) such decisions (--switch-cost).

T_BRANCH.qick = 42 | paper | arXiv:2110.00557 (QICK), latency table: "Conditional evaluation and address jump", 42 ns (16 clock cycles), original tProcessor firmware
T_BRANCH.qubic = 14 | paper | arXiv:2309.10333 (QubiC 2.0): "most register writes/control flow instructions execute in 4-7 clock cycles" at 500 MHz, so 8 to 14 ns; the upper end is used
T_BRANCH.ibm = 3450 | measured | ibm_kingston 2026-09-27, 3.45 us per if_else block, 95% CI 0.32 to 6.58 us (results/phase4_ibm.md); an x gate per block is included

  Cross-check, measured (results/qubic_dispatch.md, 2026-11-04): on the
  QubiC distributed processor RTL a conditional jump takes 6 cycles (12 ns
  at 500 MHz), taken or not, and an unconditional jump 4 cycles (8 ns). A
  compiled `if` takes both (QubiC has no not-equal), 20 ns. The sweep keeps
  T_BRANCH.qubic = 14 ns from the paper; RTL simulation, no qubits.

  Not a t_branch source: Quantum Machines OPX+ "conditional feedback
  224 ns" (vendor page, notes/prior-work.txt) is a full readout-to-pulse
  latency, which maps to t_ff.

HELIOS (Quantinuum Helios): no preset
  arXiv:2511.05465 (Helios) gives a 2q gate time of about 70 us, ground
  state cooling of about 3 ms before 2q gates, and an average of 55 ms
  per depth-1 layer (transport, cooling, and gates). It gives no 1q or
  measure duration. Selene ships no default durations (all 0). Execution
  time on Helios is dominated by batched transport and cooling, which
  this per-op model does not represent. A preset would be mostly
  assumed values, so none is added.

HERON_LIKE (the Phase 2 and 3 preset) is kept unchanged so that those
results stay reproducible. Its values were starting guesses.
